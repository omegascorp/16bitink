# Fish Survival Tournament: research (2026-11-12)

Legend. Source status: **V** = checked by web search this session (secondary or primary abstract seen). **R** = recalled from the literature, NOT re-checked, needs a source pull before it goes on screen. **?** = no defensible number found; do not stamp, use "no evidence it can" wording or drop.
Stamps: MEASURED (in a study), ESTIMATED (model/inferred), REPORTED (press, field anecdote, review citing others).
Units: US first, metric in brackets.

## 1. Rounds

| # | Round (threshold) | Mechanism, one sentence | Who passes / fails | Source + status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Stop swimming 5 min** | Ram ventilators get oxygen only by pushing water over the gills by swimming; buccal pumpers push water themselves. | FAIL: great white, shortfin mako, skipjack/bluefin tuna, mackerel, sardine/anchovy-type schoolers. PASS: nurse shark, wobbegong, frogfish, goby, scorpionfish, eel, goldfish, all bony fish with a gill pump. | Great white, whale shark, mako lack buccal muscles, so cannot pump: Britannica "Do sharks really die if they stop swimming" and Live Science (V). Nurse shark buccal pumps, rests on bottom: New England Aquarium (V). Tuna: Brown & Muir 1970, J Exp Biol (R); Roberts 1978 "Ram gill ventilation in fish" (R). Stamp: **ESTIMATED** for great white (inferred from anatomy, no one has forced an adult white to stop). "5 min" is our staging number, not a study. Only ~20-24 of ~500 shark species are obligate (V, sources differ). Note nuance: reef/lemon/tiger sharks are facultative, so "all sharks die" is a **MYTH**. |
| B | **Hot water 100 °F (38 °C)** | Warm water holds less oxygen while fish burn more; most fish hit their critical thermal maximum (CTmax) in the 90s °F. | PASS: desert pupfish (CTmax up to 112 °F / 44.6 °C), mangrove rivulus (collected across ~45-100 °F (37.8 °C) / 7-38 °C), goldfish (~106 °F / 41 °C, R), mummichog (acclimation dependent, ~104 °F (40 °C), R), Mozambique tilapia (~108 °F / 42 °C, R). FAIL: Antarctic icefish/notothenioids (upper lethal only ~43 °F / 6 °C, Somero & DeVries 1967 Science, R), Atlantic cod and salmon (die in the 70s °F, R), most reef fish (clownfish lose it in the mid-90s, R). | Pupfish 44.6 °C: Schoenherr abstract, Desert Fishes Council Proc. XLI; Lowe & Heath 1969 Physiol Zool 42:53 (V abstract only). Rivulus 7-38 °C: Taylor 2012 "Twenty-four years in the mud", PMC3501094 (V, field range = **REPORTED**, not a lab CTmax; Biology Letters 2015 PMC4650182 measured it, I did not see the number: PULL). Beitinger, Bennett & McCauley 2000 Env Biol Fish 58:237 compiles CTmax for ~116 freshwater species (R). |
| C | **Out of water 24 h, damp air** | Skin or air-breathing organs take over from the gills if the skin stays wet. | PASS: mangrove rivulus (stops gill ventilation, skin breathes; up to ~66 days), African lungfish (aestivates in a mucus cocoon, 7-8 months typical in wild, up to ~4 yr in reviews, captive 6-7 yr), mudskipper (REPORTED, lives out of water daily), European eel (REPORTED, overland on wet nights). FAIL ("no evidence it can"): goldfish, pupfish, most others. | Rivulus: Taylor guest post + Oceanbites + Practical Fishkeeping say 60-66 days (V, secondary). The 66-day figure traces to a lab study, believed Abel, Koenig & Davis 1987 Env Biol Fish 18:67 (R, PULL). Lungfish: Frontiers Physiol 2016 doi 10.3389/fphys.2016.00532 says ~4 yr (V); cocoon forms in 6-8 days; I found NO primary field study confirming 4 yr, so stamp **REPORTED**. 100-year cocoons are a **MYTH**. |
| D | **Salt switch: fresh to sea water (35 ppt, ~4.7 oz salt per gal) in one go** | Euryhaline fish swap their gill ion pumps from absorbing to excreting salt; stenohaline fish cannot. | PASS: bull shark, eel, salmon smolt (gradual), mummichog (direct transfer, Wood & Marshall 1994 Estuaries 17:34, R), mangrove rivulus (Sutton et al 2018 Copeia 106:305 abrupt-salinity study, V), desert pupfish (adults ~80-85 ppt, V secondary), tilapia. FAIL: African lungfish (freshwater only, R), goldfish (R, die in full sea water; PULL a lethal-salinity number), most freshwater fish and stenohaline reef fish put in fresh water. | Mummichog "0-120 ppt" claim NOT found in my search: V says only "0-35 ppt growth unaffected". Griffith 1974 Copeia 319 (R). Do not stamp 120. Pupfish: Kinne & Kinne 1962, Nature link and CO water-board page (V). |
| E | **Almost no oxygen, 1 h** | Air breathing, skin breathing, or anoxic metabolism. | PASS: crucian carp (months of anoxia under ice, ethanol pathway), goldfish (same pathway, same genus family), lungfish, tarpon (gulps air with swim bladder, R), rivulus. FAIL: tuna, trout/salmon, most reef fish. | Crucian carp months: Lefevre et al, Vornanen et al 2009 (V via citation chain). Ethanol >50 mg/100 mL blood: Fagernes et al 2017 Scientific Reports (V secondary). Stamp **MEASURED**. Tarpon air-breathing: pull a primary source (R). |
| F | **Hit a target above the water** (bonus/immunity) | Tongue-and-palate groove squeezed by gill covers makes a water jet. | Archerfish. Well-trained fish 100% hits within 65 cm (2.1 ft), dropping beyond; jets recorded up to ~2 m (6.6 ft) in the wild; force barely drops to 6 body lengths; accepts 45-110 deg angles. Reach of ~10 ft (3 m) is **REPORTED** only. | Schuster et al., J Exp Biol (65 cm) and Gerullis & Schuster 2014 Curr Biol, via ND "Biomechanics in the wild" blog, Wake Forest news release, Nature news (V secondary, PULL the papers). |
| G | **Glide out of water** (bonus/immunity) | Big pectoral fins as wings; tail "taxi" in the water to launch. | Flying fish: longest video duration 45 s, filmed by an NHK crew off Yakushima, Japan, May 2008 (Guinness; ~19 mph / 30 km/h from ferry speed, so ESTIMATED), beating a 1928 42 s stopwatch report. Longest distance ~1,312 ft (400 m) from Davenport 1994 review: **REPORTED**. | Guinness World Records, Live Science (V). They glide, they don't flap: "flying" is a **MYTH** of wording. |
| H | **Sprint myth-bust** (interlude, not elimination) | Water is 800x denser than air; at very high speed vapour bubbles (cavitation) form on the fins. | Claim: sailfish/marlin ~68-80 mph (110-130 km/h, popularly 35 m/s = 78 mph). Measured: ~7 m/s (15.7 mph / 25 km/h) typical burst, not over 10 m/s (22 mph / 36 km/h) in predator-prey (Marras et al 2015 Integr Comp Biol 55:719, MEASURED via accelerometer + video, V). Muscle-twitch estimate 8.3 ± 1.4 m/s (18.6 mph / 30 km/h): Svendsen et al 2016 Biol Open 5:1415 ESTIMATED (V). Stamp the old number **MYTH**. Not JEB, correct the cite from your note. |

Rejected or demoted rounds:
- **Cold, 32 °F (0 °C)**: kills the proposed winner (rivulus lower range ~45 °F / 7 °C) and the pupfish (CTmin 7 °C). Real science is good (Antarctic notothenioids stay unfrozen near 28.9 °F / -1.9 °C sea water via antifreeze glycoproteins, DeVries & Wohlschlag 1969 Science, R; goldfish/crucian survive ice-covered ponds), so use it only if you choose a cold-tolerant winner.
- **Starvation**: only lungfish (aestivation, "4 years between meals" Discover Wildlife / Guinness, REPORTED) has a headline; no clean head-to-head; skip.
- **Homing (salmon smell)**: not a pass/fail number; skip or cameo (R).
- **Pressure/depth**: no comparable threshold across the cast; skip.

Recommended 6: A swim, B heat, C land, D salt, E oxygen, then a Final. F and G as bonus/immunity cameos, H as a MYTH interlude. That makes 6 rounds + 3 cameos.

## 3. Story (proposed order)

Opening: **100 fish enter**. Illustrative counts, say so on screen (the field of 100 is staging, not data). Squid enters and is tossed (accurate).
1. **Round A stop swimming**: 100 -> 37. The great white, mako, tuna and a cloud of sardines/anchovies drift off with OUT stamps (63 out). Great white tag: ESTIMATED. Quick MYTH note: nurse shark rests. Nurse shark is the R1 winner gag.
2. **Round B 100 °F (37.8 °C) water**: clownfish, cod, salmon, eel, icefish OUT. Icefish heartbreak (they can't take 43 °F (6.1 °C)). Goldfish, pupfish, mummichog, tilapia, rivulus, lungfish, mudskipper left. ~37 -> ~12.
3. **Round C land 24 h**: goldfish, pupfish, mummichog, tilapia OUT. Lungfish celebrates (cocoon). Rivulus and mudskipper quietly pass. Honest wording for fails: "no study shows it can".
4. **Round D salt**: lungfish falls (freshwater only), fan favourite rollercoaster. Rivulus + mudskipper through.
5. **MYTH interlude H** (sailfish 78 mph claimed, 16-22 mph measured); **F archerfish** and **G flying fish** as cameos.
6. **Round E low oxygen**: both pass (air/skin breathing). Do not use it as the decider.
7. **Final head-to-head, one number: days out of water in damp air.** Rivulus ~66 days (lab, R) vs mudskipper (no number found: PULL; known as a daily commuter, not a multi-week survivor, but I did not find a source). If the mudskipper figure can't be sourced, run the final as "which fish survives longer in the same low-oxygen tide pool", or swap the finalist for the eel (REPORTED).

Winner: **mangrove rivulus** (V for 7-38 °C, salinity abrupt exposure, 60-66 days out of water; R for the lab source). Underdog arc: introduce it in the crowd at R1 as "a fish smaller than your thumb".

Backup winner if the rivulus numbers fail the source pull: desert pupfish (heat 112 °F (44.4 °C), salt 80-85 ppt) with a land round dropped or reduced to "hold breath", but then the land cameo for lungfish is lost.

## Popular claims = MYTH / unsourced candidates
- Sailfish/marlin 68-80 mph: **MYTH** (measured 16-22 mph).
- "Sharks die if they stop swimming": only partly true, ~20-24 of ~500 species are obligate.
- "Lungfish live 100 years in a cocoon": unsupported; ~4 yr in reviews, 6-7 captive.
- "Flying fish fly": they glide.
- "Goldfish 3-second memory": MYTH (off-topic, use for a caption only if wanted).
- "Mummichog survives 120 ppt": not found in my search; do not use.
- "Archerfish hits 10 ft": REPORTED only; measured near 2 m / 6.6 ft.
