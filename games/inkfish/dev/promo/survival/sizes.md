## Title / thumbnail pair

Keep ONE unit. Best: inches, since everything from 1.5 in to 180 in fits: "1.5 in vs 180 in" (rivulus vs great white). In cm: "4 cm vs 460 cm". Pounds are the most dramatic but the rivulus is a fraction of an ounce: "0.0005 lb vs 2,200 lb" reads badly, so use inches or cm. If the video starts at the goldfish-sized mudskipper as the "small" fish, use "2 in vs 180 in" (pupfish/mudskipper vs great white).

Recommended "smallest" for the opener: the desert pupfish or P. minutus at "2 in" is rounder and safer than 1.5 in (rivulus is 1-3.8 cm per Wikipedia, FishBase max 7.5 cm).

## Surprises / order notes

- Mudskipper P. minutus (max 3 in) is no bigger than a desert pupfish (2-3 in); the video's final-species mudskipper is not clearly larger than #2. P. argentilineatus (4 in typical, 7.5 in max) is much bigger, but is the wrong species.
- Goldfish (4 in) is bigger than all three tiny fish, yet its record max (19 in) overlaps the eel/icefish.
- European eel female (20 in) and blackfin icefish (20 in) tie on typical length; eel is longer by max (52 in vs 28 in), icefish is heavier-bodied.
- Atlantic salmon (29 in) is SHORTER than skipjack (31 in) and cod (39 in) typically. By weight salmon (10 lb) ~ skipjack (18-22 lb) < cod. Salmon FishBase common length is 100 cm, which would put it next to cod; NOAA's 28-30 in is the sea-run adult figure.
- Great white vs mako: the mako is 9 ft / 130-310 lb, a fifth to a tenth of a typical female great white's weight (2,200+ lb).
- Typical weights are not published for several species (goldfish, eel, icefish, cod); only maxes are.
