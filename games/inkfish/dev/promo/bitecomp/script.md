# Long-form #6: "Bite force comparison… but every number is real"

**Status:** scripted 2026-11-07, facts checked by two fact-check passes the same day (sources below).

A narrated stair-step of bites, from your own back teeth (135 lb of force) to megalodon (up to about 40,000 lb, estimated). Every step shows one number climbing, in **pounds of force** with newtons under it, and a stamp: **MEASURED** (a live animal bit a sensor) or **ESTIMATED** (a scientist's model). The twist at the end: pound for pound, the strongest bite in the video belongs to a 2.5 lb fish.

- **Format:** 1920×1080, 30 fps, not looping. Target about 4:00 (~520 words), the end card holding 5 s after the last word.
- **Voice:** the locked narrator, *Mark – Natural Conversations* (`UgBBYS2sOqTuMpoF3BR0`, `eleven_v4`), directed by "Directing the voice" in `docs/shorts-playbook.md`. Voiced in six blocks.
- **Sound:** the voice; a low bed that deepens as the bites grow; a gauge tick as the counter climbs; a hard CHOMP on every bite; a stamp thump on MEASURED/ESTIMATED.

## Why this shape

- **#2's winning title shape on a big search term.** *Fish Eat Fish Size Comparison… But Every Animal Is Real* (7.2% CTR, 20.6k views by 2026-11-07) is the long-form winner. YouTube autocomplete for "bite force" is deep: *bite force comparison*, *animal bite force comparison*, *strongest bite force*, *bite force of [lion, crocodile, human, hippo, hyena…]*, *megalodon bite force*.
- **"But every number is real" is a true angle against the genre.** Most bite lists online give "psi", which is pressure, not force, and many figures have no source (the fact-check found no measurement at all for the orca, hippo, gorilla or grizzly). Ours shows only measured numbers or estimates labelled as estimates.
- **Stair-step:** 135 → 228 → 313 → 397 → 1,330 → 2,961 → 3,690 → ~4,000 → 7,800 → ~40,000 lb, the number on screen at every step, with an everyday weight beside it ("= a person standing on one tooth", "= a small car", "= an elephant"). The low steps go fast; the top steps get the most time.
- **Open loop:** "the strongest bite for its size… belongs to a fish you could hold in one hand", planted in the cold open, re-hooked after the crocodile, closed by the piranha.
- **Arguable lines (comment test):** "Less than you'd think" (the lion), the dog's average of 58 lb, "most bite numbers online were never measured". Pinned question: *"Which number surprised you most? 🦁🐊🦈"*.

## Sources (all checked 2026-11-07)

| Step | Figure | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human | ~600 N, ~135 lbf (typical adult maximum, molars) | Measured | Varga et al. 2011, *Eur J Orthod* 33:427 |

| Dog | max 1,394 N, 313 lbf; mean 256 N, 58 lbf (22 pet dogs, 7–55 kg) | Measured | Lindner et al. 1995, *J Vet Dent* 12:49 |
| Lion | 1,768 N, ~397 lbf (canine) | Estimated (dry skull) | Wroe et al. 2005, Table 1 |
| Bull shark | up to 5,914 N, ~1,330 lbf (2.85 m shark); highest mass-specific bite of the 13 sharks and rays compared, above the white shark | Estimated (theoretical, from dissected jaw muscles) | Habegger et al. 2012, *Zoology* 115:354 |
| American alligator | 13,172 N, ~2,960 lbf | Measured | Erickson et al. 2003/2004 |
| Saltwater crocodile | 16,414 N, 3,690 lbf, about 4.5 m (15 ft); "the highest bite force measured in any animal" | Measured | Erickson et al. 2012, *PLOS ONE* 7:e31781 |
| Great white shark | "may exceed c. 1.8 tonnes" ≈ 18,000 N, ~4,000 lbf | Estimated (3D model of the skull) | Wroe et al. 2008, *J Zool* 276:336 |
| T. rex | up to 34,522 N, ~7,800 lbf; other studies higher (Bates & Falkingham: 29,510–53,735 N after their 2018 correction) | Estimated | Gignac & Erickson 2017, *Sci Rep* 7 |
| Megalodon | "about an order of magnitude greater" than the white shark; up to 182,201 N, ~41,000 lbf | Estimated | Wroe et al. 2008 (range as quoted by the Natural History Museum) |
| Black piranha | 320 N, ~72 lbf, from a 1.1 kg (2.5 lb) fish: more than 30× its weight; "the strongest bite force recorded" for a bony fish | Measured | Grubich et al. 2012, *Sci Rep* 2:1009 |
| Croc jaw opening | the jaw-opening muscles are far weaker than the closing ones; handlers tape crocodilians' jaws shut | General | Never say any adult croc can be held shut by hand |

Don't say: "psi" for any figure; "the strongest of any vertebrate" for the piranha; that megalodon's bite was weak; an orca, hippo or hyena figure.

## Script

Six blocks, voiced as six takes. Moods in brackets are ElevenLabs tags (not spoken).

### 1 · Cold open + human (0:00–0:35)

> [dramatically] This is the strongest bite ever MEASURED. [excited] Three thousand, six hundred and ninety pounds of force… from one crocodile. [curious] But a lot of bite numbers online? Nobody ever measured them. [sarcastic] Half of them are in "psi"… which isn't even a bite. [excited] So today: only REAL numbers. Measured… or clearly marked as an estimate. [mischievously] And the strongest bite for its size? It belongs to a fish you could hold in one hand. [softly] Hold onto that. [curious] Let's start with you. Clench your back teeth on a sensor, and an adult bites about a hundred and thirty-five pounds. [chuckles] That's like a whole person… standing on one tooth.

*On screen:* the croc's jaws slamming on a sensor bar, the counter racing to 3,690, MEASURED stamp; a scroll of "psi" lists getting crossed out; a fish silhouette in a hand with "?"; then a human jaw on the sensor, counter 135, a stick person standing on one molar.

### 2 · Jaguar, dog, lion (0:35–1:20)

> [excited] Now a jaguar. Scientists modelled its skull: about two hundred and thirty pounds at the fangs. [surprised] Enough to crack a turtle's SHELL. [curious] A big pet dog? Researchers had twenty-two dogs chew a sensor. The hardest bite: three hundred and thirteen pounds. [chuckles] The average? Just fifty-eight. [dramatically] And a lion: about four hundred pounds, estimated. [sarcastic] Less than you'd think… because a lion doesn't crush. It holds on.

*On screen:* each animal bites the bar in turn; ESTIMATED / MEASURED / ESTIMATED stamps; a turtle shell cracking; 22 little dogs in a grid, one glowing; the lion's long fangs.

### 3 · Game mention + bull shark (1:20–2:05)

> [warmly] Quick one: this channel makes InkFish, a free game where you start tiny… and bite your way up the food chain. [excited] Speaking of which: the numbers are about to JUMP. [dramatically] A bull shark: up to thirteen hundred pounds, worked out from its jaw muscles. [surprised] For its weight, that's the strongest bite of every shark they compared… even the great white.

*On screen:* a quick InkFish gameplay cut (a tiny fish growing), "16bit.ink" small; the counter leaps 397 → 1,330; the bull shark's blunt head; a row of shark silhouettes with the bull shark's bar the tallest "for its weight".

### 4 · Alligator, crocodile (2:05–2:55)

> [dramatically] An American alligator, biting a real sensor: two thousand, nine hundred and sixty pounds. [chuckles] About the weight of a small CAR. [excited] And the record. A saltwater crocodile, about fifteen feet long: three thousand, six hundred and ninety pounds! [surprised] The strongest bite ever measured… in ANY animal. [mischievously] Funny thing, though: the muscles that OPEN those jaws are far weaker. That's why handlers can tape them shut. [curious] Remember that fish you could hold in one hand? We're getting closer.

*On screen:* the alligator on the bar, a small car on the gauge's scale; the croc, the counter to 3,690, a gold RECORD stamp; a handler taping a small croc's snout; the fish-in-hand silhouette again.

### 5 · Great white, T. rex, megalodon (2:55–3:40)

> [dramatically] Now the estimates get HUGE. A great white shark: about four thousand pounds, from a 3D model of its skull. [excited] Tyrannosaurus rex: up to seven thousand, eight hundred pounds. [surprised] Some studies go even higher. [chuckles] Either way… that's an elephant, on one tooth. [dramatically] And megalodon. Scale up the great white, and you get roughly TEN times the bite. [excited] Up to about FORTY thousand pounds… [softly] an estimate, from fossil teeth and a giant model shark.

*On screen:* the great white's jaws as a wireframe model then painted; T. rex bites the bar, an elephant on one tooth; megalodon's jaws fill the frame, the counter racing to ~40,000, ESTIMATED.

### 6 · The twist: the piranha (3:40–4:10)

> [surprised] But for its SIZE? Nothing here comes close to THIS. [excited] A black piranha. Two and a half pounds. It bit a sensor at seventy-two pounds… [dramatically] more than THIRTY times its own weight! [curious] The strongest bite ever recorded for a bony fish. [mischievously] So pound for pound, the scariest bite in this video… fits in one hand. [warmly] And if you like starting small and biting above your weight… InkFish is free, at 16bit.ink.

*On screen:* the line-up of every animal pulls back; the piranha swims in tiny, bites the bar, the counter shows 72 and then "×30 its weight" outscaling everything; a hand holding the piranha; the end card.
