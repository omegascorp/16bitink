# Fact-check: "What's the biggest thing a snake can swallow?" (2026-11-03)

Primary papers were not fully readable. Read: the Jayne 2024 abstract (journals.ku.edu), UC News 2023 and 2024, Nat Geo (hyena, croc), phys.org. Not read: the Herpetological Review 2018 paper, the Jayne 2023 abstract (details via secondary reports), Fredriksson 2005 full text, and JSTOR Daily (404). Verdicts: OK / SOFTEN (true but overstated or loose) / FIX (wrong or misleading as spoken).

| Claim | Verdict | Source | Replacement wording |
|---|---|---|---|

| Hunts mice around barns and fields | OK | ncwildlife.gov; common in grain stores and farms | none |
| Adult mouse "about an ounce" | OK | keeper figures 20-30 g; 1 oz = 28 g | Counter "1 oz · 28 g" matches the line better than "0.06 lb · 25 g" |
| Belly looks like a cob of corn | SOFTEN | NC Wildlife says the name likely comes from the belly resembling Indian corn; other sources say cornfields and grain stores. Contested. | "Fun fact: some say it's named for its belly… flip one over, and the checkers look like a cob of Indian corn." |

| "each side walks forward on its own" | SOFTEN | The alternating "walk" or ratchet is described mainly for the upper jaw bones (palatopterygoid, "pterygoid walk"). The lower jaw halves also move independently. Sources disagree on which does the walking. | "The two halves are tied by stretchy skin, so they spread apart… and the jaws take turns, creeping forward over the prey, one side at a time." Inset: show both upper and lower jaws moving, not only the lower |
| Windpipe pushed "out the front, like a snorkel" | OK (not primary) | General herpetology: the glottis extends forward, even past the lips, so the snake keeps breathing. Sources found were secondary; JSTOR Daily 404. | "it pushes its windpipe forward, like a SNORKEL!" ("forward" is safer than "out the front") |
| "First, forget unhinging" / "none unhinge" | OK | UC 2024: lower jaw is not fused, so no dislocation is needed | none |
| Naples: "scientists found a fifteen-foot python swallowing a 77-lb deer" | OK | Jayne 2024 abstract: one snake "captured while eating a deer"; press: Bartoszek and Easterling found it mid-swallow, ~30 min to finish after they arrived. 77 lb = 34.9 kg. | "almost fifteen-foot" is safer than rounding up (14.8 ft); name card keeps "14.8 ft" |
| Gape "about ten inches across" | OK | Jayne 2024: 26 cm = 10.2 in | none |
| "the widest gape ever measured in a python" | FIX | Three Burmese pythons all measured 26 cm; the previous reported maximum was 22 cm. The claim is about Burmese pythons, not all pythons. | "the widest gape ever measured in a Burmese python" |
| "a python with a mouth a foot wide… a deer of about 120 lb" | SOFTEN | Jayne (UC News): "very plausible that a record-breaking python with a gape of 30 cm could eat a 120-pound deer" (30 cm = 11.8 in). A projection, not a case. | "A scientist says a python with a mouth about a foot wide could get down a deer of about a hundred and twenty pounds." |
| Heart grows "up to forty percent… just to digest it" | OK | Secor: heart mass up ~40% within about 3 days (sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/10/111027145900.htm; Riquelme 2011, Science 334:528). The 40% is from press quotes. | "a python's heart can grow by about forty percent within days… to power digestion" ("just to digest it" is fine) |
| Hyena: Kenya 2017, Masai Mara, tourist filmed rock python swallowing a hyena | OK | nationalgeographic.com/animals/article/snakes-pythons-africa-hyena-kill: Jos Bakker (Dutch tourist) filmed it; the video shows the swallowing; researchers found the sated snake the next morning. Nat Geo: first-ever video | none |
| ~13 ft python, ~150 lb hyena | SOFTEN | Nat Geo: both are estimates (13 ft; 150 lb / 68 kg) | "About thirteen feet of snake… an estimated hundred and fifty pounds of hyena." Keep "≈" on screen |

| "the heaviest meal we could find on camera" | OK | No heavier well-documented case found. Impala ~130 lb (rock python, South Africa, 1955; secondary sources). Reticulated python and sun bear (Fredriksson 2005): snake ~7 m, bear ~23 kg per one secondary source, lighter and found via a radio collar, not on camera. Nat Geo's python list has nothing above 150 lb. | Keep "heaviest we could find on camera" (honest hedge) |
| "In 2023, a scientist measured how wide snakes can open their mouths, compared with how big they are" | FIX | Jayne 2023 (J. Zoology) studied one egg-eater species (15 Gans' egg-eaters, 20-90 cm) against the black rat snake and published data. It was not a survey of "snakes". | "In 2023, a scientist measured how wide a little African snake can open its mouth, compared with how big it is." |
| "the winner was THIS" / biggest mouth in the snake world | SOFTEN | Jayne: egg-eater's prey is 3-4x bigger than a generalist's; "probably would hold the Guinness world record". Not a ranking of all snakes. | "…and the winner, probably, was THIS: the Gans' egg-eater." End line: "So the biggest mouth for its size… probably belongs to one of the smaller snakes." |
| Gans' egg-eater: "three-foot snake from Africa that eats nothing but eggs" | OK | UC 2023: grow to about three feet; egg specialist; largest measured ~94 cm | none |
| "Head less than half an inch wide… can swallow something two inches across" | SOFTEN | Largest snake: head ~1 cm (0.4 in), could take a ~5 cm (2 in) cylinder. | "More than TWICE what a Burmese python can fit, for its weight" | OK | UC 2023 and phys.org: prey with "more than twice the cross-sectional area of a Burmese python of similar weight" | "For its weight, it can take in more than TWICE the size a Burmese python can!" ("area" is the exact measure; "size" is fine for narration) |
| "Spikes on its backbone crack the egg in its throat… spits out the shell, folded flat" | OK | Standard Dasypeltis biology (hypapophyses); the shell is crushed and regurgitated. Not re-checked this session. | "spits out the shell, crushed flat" ("folded" is less sure) |
| "one of the smallest snakes" | SOFTEN | ~3 ft is small for a snake but not among the tiniest (threadsnakes are under a foot). | "belongs to a snake barely three feet long" or "to a pretty small snake" |

## Also noted
- The cold-open "barely three feet" (Gans' maximum ~94 cm = 3.1 ft) is fine.
- Name card "OLIVE PYTHON · ≈ 10 ft": Nat Geo and the news say 10 ft.
- Map pin "Collier County · 2015": Collier-Seminole State Park is in Collier County, fine. For the deer pin, press dates are unclear (collected in 2023 or earlier, published Oct 2024), so leave the year off.
- Pinned comment and "Nobody was laughing" are fine.
