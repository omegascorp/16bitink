# What is the biggest thing a snake can swallow? Research (2026-11-03)

Method: web search plus page fetches (news, university and museum press, Wikipedia as a pointer only). The primary papers were NOT readable (Wiley returned 403; the Raffles PDF and Iguana PDF did not extract). Every number below is therefore from press coverage of the papers unless marked otherwise. Estimates are labelled EST. Nothing here comes from the game's own research doc except the species list.

## Recommended ladder (meal weight strictly rising)

| 4 | Burmese python, 4.5 m (14.8 ft), 115.2 lb (52.3 kg) | adult white-tailed deer | 76.9 lb (34.9 kg) = 67% of the snake | 2.2x | measured, published (Reptiles & Amphibians 2024) |
| 5 | African rock python, ~4 m (13 ft) | spotted hyena | ~150 lb (68 kg) EST | ~2x | filmed; weight is an outlet estimate; swallow was in progress |
| 6 TWIST | Gans' egg-eater, ~0.9 m (3 ft) | bird egg, many times wider than its head | tiny in pounds, record by size | weight DROPS | peer-reviewed (Jayne 2023), exact egg size not found |

Notes on the ladder:
- Step 2 is the weakest (all EST). If you want a firmer rung, swap for nothing: there is no measured meal between a mouse and a 35 lb fawn among the game snakes. Keep it, but say "about" and "an expert estimated".
- Several steps are under 3x (4 and 5). Weight cannot be stretched; the honest framing is "snakes get close to their own body weight".
- Step 3 to 4 reads well as "bigger snake, bigger deer". The 111% fawn is the stronger ratio; the 77 lb deer is the heavier meal.
- Step 6 breaks the rule on purpose: switch the axis from pounds to "size of meal compared with the eater". Do not show a pounds number for the egg.
- Game species used: corn snake, egg-eater (Gans' egg-eater is a close relative of the game's common egg-eater, Dasypeltis scabra). Sea krait, king cobra, anaconda: no weight-documented meal found (see below). Say "from the same family as" for the egg-eater if the video shows the game snake.

## Facts that changed from the brief

1. The 77 lb deer python is NOT in Big Cypress and NOT the "heaviest prey-to-snake ratio". It is southwestern Florida near Naples (one outlet says December 2022; another says Everglades National Park; no source gives Big Cypress; the Big Cypress deer was a different snake: 61 lb deer, vomited after a cold snap). Ratio is 67% of snake mass. The 2018 fawn case (111%) is the higher ratio and was called "the largest predator to prey ratio ever documented for the Burmese python" by the Conservancy. The 77 lb deer is the heaviest deer, and the paper's gape is the widest measured: 26 cm (10.2 in).
2. Paper is "Big pythons, big gape, and big prey", Jayne, Easterling and Bartoszek, Reptiles & Amphibians 31(1), e21867, Aug-Oct 2024 (journals.ku.edu). Not Beyer et al. 2023.
3. Olive python and croc are small: croc ~1 m, 5-7 lb, python ~3 m. It is a cute rung, not a record.
4. The African rock python hyena is real and filmed (Masai Mara, March 2017) and is the heaviest well-sourced meal found; the weight is an estimate.
5. Egg-eater: no snake mass or egg mass published in any press source. The ">2x a Burmese python" is by cross-sectional area at the SAME WEIGHT, not the same length (see section 9).
6. King cobra: no weight-documented meal. Largest "python" record is a 1947 attempt (3.66 m cobra, 2.79 m python), unverified whether finished.

## 9. THE TWIST: Gans' egg-eater (Dasypeltis gansi)
| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Paper | B. C. Jayne (2023), "Scaling relationships of maximal gape and prey size of snakes for an egg-eating specialist (Dasypeltis gansi) and a dietary generalist (Pantherophis obsoletus)", Journal of Zoology, DOI 10.1111/jzo.13102. UC News 24 Aug 2023 |
| Snakes measured | 15 Gans' egg-eaters, 21 to 94 cm (8 to 37 in); largest ~94 cm (3 ft) (Science News Explores). Mass NOT in any press source |
| Gape | Largest snake: head ~1 cm wide, could swallow a 5 cm (2 in) wide cylinder. Smallest: 1.4 cm. A Burmese python of the SAME LENGTH: 4.4 cm |
| Headline numbers | Takes prey with more than 2x the cross-sectional area of a Burmese python OF SIMILAR WEIGHT; prey 3-4x bigger than a generalist (black rat snake). Jayne: "They probably would hold the Guinness world record" |
| Soft tissue | ~50% of max gape is stretch (about 40% in Burmese pythons). Skin between the lower jaw halves stretches |
| Ranking | Of 13 species measured (of 3,500+), the egg-eater is first; the Burmese python and the western diamondback rattlesnake come next |
| Egg cracking | Swallowed whole; spine bone projections (hypapophyses, vertebrae 29-38 in Dasypeltis, Brecko and Herrel) crack the shell in the throat; contents go down, the crushed shell is spat out. Rat snakes digest egg and shell |
| NOT found | egg size or egg mass, snake mass. Gartner and Greene SICB abstract: meal-to-snake mass ratio 0.54 for Dasypeltis (definition unclear, date unclear; do not use) |
| Confidence | Peer reviewed; I read press, not the paper. The same-length (4.4 cm vs 5 cm) and same-weight (2x area) comparisons differ; use "same weight" for the 2x claim |

Script caution: the comparison is about relative size. "Many times wider than its head" is quoted from UC. Do not state an egg weight or "bigger than the snake".

Sources: https://www.uc.edu/news/articles/2023/08/little-african-snake-can-swallow-biggest-prey-relative-to-its-size.html , https://phys.org/news/2023-08-african-snake-swallow-biggest-prey.html , https://www.snexplores.org/?p=195200 , https://sicb.org/?p=34496 , https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/08/230825122009.htm , https://reptilesmagazine.com/gans-egg-eating-snake-can-eat-prey-twice-that-of-burmese-python-relative-to-size-and-mass/
General Dasypeltis facts (game doc, African Snake Bite Institute, Smithsonian Magazine): eggs only; rasps scales like a saw-scaled viper when scared.

## 10. How snakes do it
| Fact | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Jaws do not unhinge or dislocate | VERIFIED (Kardong; JSTOR Daily) | https://daily.jstor.org/how-snakes-swallow/ |
| Lower jaw halves are not fused at the front, joined by a stretchy ligament and skin; each side moves on its own | VERIFIED (JSTOR Daily; UC News: "not fused at the front") | same, and UC 2024 |
| Quadrate bone is long and mobile, works as a hinge that swings the jaw out | Plausible, secondary sources only | scienceinsights.org/do-snakes-have-jaws-the-anatomy-explained/ |
| Windpipe opening (glottis) pushes forward, like a snorkel, so they breathe while swallowing | Secondary sources agree; not confirmed in a paper | daily.jstor.org, enviroliteracy.org |
| Heart grows ~40% after a big meal (Burmese python), within ~2-3 days, shrinks afterward | Press attributes to Secor; Riquelme et al. 2011 Science 334:528 is about heart-cell growth (cells enlarge, not multiply). The 40% is NOT in the paper's abstract; it comes from Secor's mass data and press. Say "up to about 40%" | https://www.nationalgeographic.com/science/article/a-recipe-for-growing-bigger-hearts-found-in-the-blood-of-pythons , https://sicb.org/?p=28682 |

| Intestine roughly doubles in mass | Secor group | same |
| Large snakes can fast for long periods after a big meal | Not checked, not claimed |

- Burmese python with cow or calf (Indonesia or India): no credible measured source.
- Python and alligator, Everglades 2005: real but exploded and no weights (gore rule).
- Python and human cases: skipped by instruction.
- Impala 59 kg, 4.9 m snake: repeated but untraced; leave out or mark "reported".
- Hyena as a firm weight: keep as "about 150 lb" and say "estimated".
- Anaconda caiman or capybara weights: none documented.
