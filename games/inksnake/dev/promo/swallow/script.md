The first long video for InkSnake. A narrated stair-step of real snakes, each with the heaviest meal it has been documented swallowing whole, from a corn snake's mouse (about an ounce) to an African rock python's hyena (about 150 lb). The twist: measured against its own size, the record holder is a three-foot egg-eater, a cousin of the game's chapter-four snake.

- **Format:** 1920×1080, 30 fps, not looping. Target about 5:00 (~800 words), with the end card holding 5 s after the last word.
- **Voice:** the locked narrator, *Mark – Natural Conversations* (`UgBBYS2sOqTuMpoF3BR0`, `eleven_v4`), directed by "Directing the voice" in `docs/shorts-playbook.md`. One section per take.
- **Sound:** the voice; a low bed that deepens with every step; a scale clunk and a counter tick as each weight counts up; the game's swallow gulp on each lump; a heartbeat under the python's heart.
- **Captions:** an `.srt` built from the word timings, uploaded as CC (not burned in).

## Why this shape

- **The narrated swallow is the channel's best format** (top four shorts; InkSnake's krait and python shorts are the game's best), and **"Size Comparison" is the long-form engine** (#2: 6.56% CTR, 94K impressions). This puts the swallow inside the size comparison, for the game whose whole verb is swallowing.
- **One question, every section pays it off:** *what's the biggest thing a snake can swallow?* The answer climbs every step, then flips at the end.
- **MrBeast's stair-step:** the meal's weight is the number, in pounds (kg under), on screen at every step: 0.06 → ~6 → 35 → 77 → ~150 lb. The low steps go fast; the deer and hyena get the most time. The cold open previews the top (the deer, the hyena), then drops to the mouse.
- **Open loops:** (1) "the real champion is barely three feet long", planted in the cold open, closed by the egg-eater; (2) the 77-lb deer from frame 1, closed at step 5 ("remember the deer from the start?"); (3) "heavier than itself", the re-hook straight after the game mention.
- **A myth to bust:** "none of them unhinge their jaws" (cold open), paid off at the fawn.
- **The game mention** comes after two meals (~1:25), with the fawn's "heavier than itself" as the re-hook. The full pitch is at the end, after the twist, and it lands on the egg-eater being in the game.
- **Arguable lines (comment test):** "Of course it was Australia.", "Florida's deer are thrilled.", "Nobody was laughing." Pinned question: *"Which surprised you more: a python swallowing something heavier than itself, or a 3-ft snake beating it? 🦌 or 🥚"*.
- **No gore** ([[no-gross-food]] spirit): every meal goes in whole and is shown as a lump with an x-ray outline; no blood, no bursting, no human cases.

## Picture

- **One ground, one line-up.** A top-down strip of the game's own painted ground, changing biome as it goes: the farm (corn snake), a lake shore (olive python), a Florida swamp (both Burmese pythons), the savanna (rock python, then the egg-eater). Every snake comes in from the right as it does in the game, swallows its meal, and stays in the row with its lump. The camera zooms out in log space so each new pair fills the frame, then pulls back to show the row.
- **The meal counter, our "$1… $X":** big, bottom centre, **in pounds** with kg under ("35 lb · 15.9 kg"), counting up with a tick from the last meal to the new one; "≈" on estimates.
- **The lump:** the game's lump going down the body, with the meal drawn inside it as a thin pale x-ray outline (legs folded), so it reads as whole and never as gore.
- **The balance:** a little two-pan scale, snake on one side and meal on the other, for the fawn (tips to the fawn: 111%) and the adult deer (67%).
- **Name card** for every snake: "BURMESE PYTHON · 11 ft · 31.5 lb".
- **Map pins** for the places: Lake Moondarra (Australia), near Naples (Florida), Masai Mara (Kenya), with the year.
- Something new on screen every 10–15 s; every snake enters head first.

## Script

Each section gives the line as it will be voiced, the moods, then what's on screen.

### 0 · Cold open (0:00–0:28)

> [excited] This python is swallowing a DEER. A whole one! [nervous] Seventy-seven pounds… [dramatically] and it's not even the biggest meal in this video. [curious] Today we're lining up real snakes by the biggest meals they've been caught swallowing, [mischievously] from a mouse… all the way to a hyena. [chuckles] And no, none of them unhinge their jaws. [dramatically] But the real champion, at the very end? It's barely three feet long.

*Moods: shock → dread → build → curious → sly → cheeky → ominous.*

*Frame 1 is action: top-down, a huge python body fills the frame with an x-ray deer outline inside a lump, the head still stretched round the back half; the counter slams "77 lb". On "not even the biggest", a dark hyena-shaped lump slides past behind it. On "from a mouse… to a hyena", a fast fly-by of the whole line-up, small to huge. On "unhinge", a jaw diagram gets a red ✗. On "barely three feet", the camera drops to a small dark snake shape coiled round an egg, unnamed.*

### 1 · Corn snake and a mouse, ~0.06 lb (0:28–0:52)

> [warmly] Let's start small: the corn snake. [curious] About three to four feet long, and it hunts mice around barns and fields. [chuckles] One adult mouse weighs about an ounce. [mischievously] And some say it's named for its belly: flip it over, and the checkers look like a cob of corn. [excited] Okay. Let's go up… a LOT.

*Moods: cosy → curious → light → sly → hype.*

*The farm ground. The corn snake (the game's art) slides in from the right, head first, and swallows a mouse (the game's mouse); a small lump slides down. Counter: "0.06 lb" with "1 oz · 28 g" under it. On "belly", an inset of the black-and-white chequered belly beside a cob of corn. Name card: "CORN SNAKE · 4 ft".*

Sources: corn snake adults ~2.5–4 ft (most), record 6.2 ft; over half its diet rodents (Florida Museum, SREL); adult mouse ~24–30 g (keeper figure); belly chequer and the name (Smithsonian National Zoo; the other story is corn cribs).

### 2 · Olive python and a crocodile, ~6 lb (0:52–1:25)

> [surprised] In 2014, at a lake in Australia, people watched an olive python swallow a CROCODILE! [nervous] It coiled around it… squeezed… and then took it down head first, over several hours. [curious] A young freshwater croc: about three feet long, and maybe five or six pounds. [sarcastic] A snake eating a crocodile… [chuckles] of course it was Australia.

*Moods: shock → dread → curious → sarcastic → cheeky.*

*Map pin: "Lake Moondarra, Queensland · 2014". The lake-shore ground (the python short's top-down art and the game's croc). The olive python coils round the croc (the game's coil), then swallows; a long lump with the croc's x-ray outline. Counter ticks up to "≈ 6 lb · 2.5 kg". Name card: "OLIVE PYTHON · ≈ 10 ft". The camera pulls back: the corn snake is tiny at the left.*

Sources: Lake Moondarra, near Mount Isa, Queensland, 2 March 2014, photos by passers-by (ABC North West, National Geographic); python ~3 m (10 ft), croc ~1 m (3 ft) and 2–3 kg (5–7 lb), all estimates from photos by Terry Phillip (Reptile Gardens); constriction, then head first; outlets say 4–5 hours ("several hours").

> [warmly] Quick one: every snake you're seeing is drawn for a game called InkSnake. You play ten real snakes, and each one hunts its own way. [excited] Chapter one, the corn snake, is free in your browser at sixteen-bit dot ink!

*The game's farm level: the corn snake coils round a rat and swallows it. "16bit.ink" in the corner, no URL on screen beyond the name.*

### 4 · Burmese python and a fawn, 35 lb (1:40–2:30)

> [nervous] Next: a python in Florida… [dramatically] that swallowed something HEAVIER than itself. [surprised] In 2015, an eleven-foot Burmese python was found with a deer fawn inside it. The snake weighed thirty-one pounds… the fawn weighed thirty-FIVE! [curious] So how does that fit? [mischievously] First, forget unhinging. [softly] A snake's lower jaw isn't joined at the chin. The two halves are tied by stretchy skin, so they spread apart… [curious] and the jaws take turns, creeping forward over the prey, one side at a time. [surprised] And so it doesn't suffocate, it pushes its windpipe forward, like a SNORKEL!

*Moods: dread → shock → surprise → curious → sly → calm → shock.*

*Re-hook. Map pin: "Collier County, Florida · 2015". The swamp ground. The python with the fawn's x-ray lump. Counter "35 lb · 15.9 kg". The balance: "SNAKE 31.5 lb" vs "FAWN 35 lb", and it tips to the fawn: "111%". On "lower jaw", an inset: a snake skull from below, the two lower jaw halves drifting apart with a stretchy band between them, then the upper and lower jaws on one side creep forward, then the other. On "SNORKEL", the windpipe tube pokes out under the prey.*

Sources: female Burmese python, 11 ft (3.4 m), 31.5 lb (14.3 kg), with a 35 lb (15.9 kg) white-tailed fawn, Collier-Seminole State Park, found 7 April 2015, Conservancy of Southwest Florida, *Herpetological Review* (2018), "the largest predator-to-prey ratio documented" for the species (CBS Miami). Jaws: the lower jaw halves aren't fused at the front, joined by stretchy tissue, and move separately (Kardong via JSTOR Daily; UC News 2024); the glottis is pushed forward to breathe while swallowing (JSTOR Daily; to confirm).

### 5 · Burmese python and a deer, 77 lb (2:30–3:20)

> [dramatically] Remember the deer from the start? [excited] Near Naples, Florida, scientists found an almost fifteen-foot python swallowing a SEVENTY-SEVEN pound deer! [curious] They measured its mouth: about ten inches across… the widest gape ever measured in a Burmese python. [nervous] And one scientist says a python with a mouth about a foot wide could get down a deer of about a hundred and twenty pounds. [sarcastic] Florida's deer are thrilled. [surprised] Oh, and after a meal that big, a python's heart can grow by up to FORTY percent… [softly] just to digest it.

*Moods: callback → hype → curious → dread → sarcastic → shock → calm.*

*Closes loop 2: the frame-1 shot again, now named. Map pin: "Near Naples, Florida". Counter rolls 35 → "77 lb · 34.9 kg". The balance: "SNAKE 115 lb" vs "DEER 77 lb", "67%". On "ten inches", a tape measure across the open mouth, "10.2 in · 26 cm". On "a foot wide", a ghost deer outline grows to "≈ 120 lb". On "heart", an inset heart beating, swelling by 40% (heartbeat SFX). Name card: "BURMESE PYTHON · 14.8 ft · 115 lb". A big zoom out over the row.*

Sources: Jayne, Easterling & Bartoszek, "Big pythons, big gape, and big prey", *Reptiles & Amphibians* 31(1) e21867 (2024); Conservancy of Southwest Florida and UC News (Oct 2024): 14.8 ft (4.5 m), 115.2 lb (52.3 kg) female, 76.9 lb (34.9 kg) white-tailed deer, max gape 26 cm (10.2 in), previous record 22 cm; a 30 cm gape could take a ~120 lb deer (modelled). Say "found… swallowing", not "hunted" (one outlet says the deer may have been dead). Heart: up to ~40% heavier after a meal (Secor, via National Geographic; Riquelme et al. 2011, *Science*).

### 6 · African rock python and a hyena, ~150 lb (3:20–4:00)

> [dramatically] But the heaviest meal we could find on camera… isn't in Florida. [nervous] Kenya, 2017. In the Masai Mara, a tourist filmed an African rock python… [surprised] swallowing a whole spotted HYENA! [curious] About thirteen feet of snake… and an estimated hundred and fifty pounds of hyena. [chuckles] Hyenas are famous for laughing. [sarcastic] This one wasn't.

*Moods: build → dread → shock → curious → cheeky → deadpan.*

*Map pin: "Masai Mara, Kenya · 2017". The savanna ground (the game's savanna art). The rock python with a hyena's x-ray lump, the biggest lump yet. Counter rolls to "≈ 150 lb · 68 kg". Name card: "AFRICAN ROCK PYTHON · ≈ 13 ft". The biggest zoom out: the whole row, the corn snake a speck at the left.*

Sources: National Geographic (15 March 2017): video by Jos Bakker, Masai Mara, Kenya, late February 2017; a ~13 ft (4 m) African rock python swallowing a large spotted hyena of ~150 lb (68 kg) (an estimate); researchers from Kay Holekamp's hyena project (Michigan State) went back to find the snake.

### 7 · The twist: the egg-eater (4:00–4:48)

> [mischievously] So the biggest snake wins… right? [dramatically] Not even close. [curious] In 2023, a scientist measured how wide a little African snake can open its mouth, compared with how big it is. [surprised] And it might just be the world record holder: THIS, the Gans' egg-eater. A three-foot snake that eats nothing but eggs. [excited] Its head is only about half an inch wide… and its mouth can open round something two inches across! [surprised] For its weight, that's more than TWICE what a Burmese python can fit! [curious] Then spikes on its backbone crack the egg inside its throat… [chuckles] and it spits out the shell, crushed flat. [warmly] So the biggest mouth for its size… probably belongs to a snake barely three feet long.

*Moods: sly → twist → curious → shock → hype → shock → curious → cheeky → warm.*

*Closes loop 1. The camera drops from the giant row back down to the left end of the savanna: the small snake from the cold open, coiled round an egg. Counter flips from pounds to a new label: "MOUTH vs BODY". The egg-eater side-on (the egg-eater short's art), jaws stretched round the egg. On "less than half an inch", a ruler: head "½ in · 1 cm", mouth "2 in · 5 cm". On "TWICE", two circles: "Burmese python, same weight" and the egg-eater's, more than twice the area. On "spikes on its backbone", an x-ray of the neck: the spines push into the throat, crack. On "spits out the shell", the folded shell drops out. On "barely three feet long", pull back to the whole row: the egg-eater gets a gold "#1".*

Sources: Jayne (2023), *Journal of Zoology*, doi:10.1111/jzo.13102; UC News (24 Aug 2023): 15 *Dasypeltis gansi* measured, largest ~94 cm (3 ft), head ~1 cm wide, could swallow a 5 cm (2 in) cylinder; prey of more than twice the cross-sectional area of a Burmese python of similar weight (by weight, not length); "probably would hold the Guinness world record". Egg-eaters eat only eggs; vertebral projections (hypapophyses) crack the shell in the throat; the shell is regurgitated (Brecko & Herrel; the game's research doc). The game's chapter-four snake is the common egg-eater, *Dasypeltis scabra*, a close cousin.
