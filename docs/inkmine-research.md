# InkMine: mechanics research

Fifteen mining games, researched October 2026, and what InkMine takes from each. Pocket Mine 3 facts come mostly from the Roofdog Games wiki (built from game data). Pocket Mine 1 and 2 facts come from store listings, reviews and tips sites. Facts marked *(unverified)* are from memory, not a source read during the research.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Pocket Mine** (2013, mobile) | Pick strength: every tap spends one hit | Crates (bomb, dynamite, drill, gas) that make a few taps pay big | ✅ Energy spent per block, ✅ crates |
| 2 | **Pocket Mine 2** (2014) | Same, plus energy to start runs | Islands of 5 levels with a depth goal each; gear with powers; no monsters (praised) | ✅ A goal per level; ❌ energy |

Also checked: Wall World and Keep Digging (mining called dull or buggy), Deep Town and Idle Miner Tycoon (pacing built for ads), Diggy's Adventure (energy per tile as a paywall), and the well-rated small premium miners Underminer (2024) and DigDigDrill (2024). Those two show that a plain dig, upgrade, descend loop still sells at a premium price.

## Pocket Mine in detail

The favourite, so its mechanics are kept apart per version.

**Pocket Mine 1 (2013)**
- Tap blocks next to the miner to dig down an endless mine; the miner drops as the ground opens.
- Each tap costs one hit of pick strength, and hard blocks take several taps. The run ends at zero, so players learn to skip dirt and go for ore and crates.
- Blocks are revealed only around open space.
- Before a run you get 3 booster cards ("more coal", "more bomb crates"), with a paid reshuffle. Duplicates fuse into stronger cards.
- Items: bomb crates, gas, grenades, drills, and dynamite that clears a whole row. Boxes hold gold or artifacts; chests hold cards or rubies.
- One mission per dig gives keys. A museum collects artifact sets.
- Complaints: energy to start runs (5 to begin, refilling in real time), loot boxes, ads.

**Pocket Mine 2 (2014–15)**
- Islands unlock in sequence, each with 5 levels with a depth goal each, about 27 islands.
- Same pick hits. Pick upgrades got extremely expensive (about 1M cash for 210 hits).
- New blocks:
  - Bank blocks: one hit, release cash.
  - Fireworks: bounce at angles.
  - Gas crates: fill nearby space with gas that explodes after about 3 s.
  - Worm crates: release a worm that burrows down and explodes.
- Gear with powers, for example the Alien Eye Piece that shows ore within 5 blocks.
- Pets. Factories turn artifacts into cards on real-time timers.
- Special levels: Galore, Labyrinth, Dilemma (a chest every 60 m).
- No monsters, which reviewers praised.
- Complaints: a challenge that comes "from being free-to-play rather than actually being difficult", paying to revive a broken pick, energy waits, popups.

**Pocket Mine 3 (2017, still updated)**
- 67 worlds of 5 levels each, from Plains to the Moon, each with its own monsters. A mine is a vertical column with an idol at the bottom. Smash the idol to finish; a broken pick ends the run.
- One durability per hit:
  - Dirt 1 HP, rock 2 HP.
  - Coal and iron 1 HP, gold and diamond 2 HP.
  - Crates 1 HP; they fall when unsupported.
  - "Rigged" ore explodes.
- Ore goes into a bag and turns to cash at the end.
- Before a run you take a digger card plus booster cards (51 cards in four families), with paid rerolls. Each digger has a special power that charges as you hit blocks.
- Gear slots: pick, hat, gloves, boots, amulet, ring.
- Monsters roam dark areas and notice you within 4 blocks. Most deal 1 damage, big ones 3. Explosions kill groups.
- Complaints: longtime fans felt monsters "didn't need to be added" and that the game is cluttered. Also stamina, auto-scroll, and duplicate artifacts with no use.

**The thing to steal:** one finite resource spent per hit makes every hit a decision, and crates let a few smart hits set off big chain reactions. The skill is spending hits well.

## Lessons

**Worth adapting**

1. Finite pick hits per trip, raised by upgrades. Present it as wear on the pick, never as energy to start a run.
2. Block hardness you can read by sight, with harder rock gated by tool tier.
3. Chain-reaction crates that fall when unsupported.
4. Cargo-limited round trips and the "turn back now?" call.
5. Underground outposts so big maps don't turn into trudging.
6. A pass/fail goal per level plus optional bonus objectives.
7. A small loadout of three gadgets, chosen, never dealt at random.
8. Five or six upgrade lines with distinct effects.
9. A streak bonus for mining a whole vein.
10. Hand-made treasure rooms off a guaranteed main path.

**To avoid**

- Energy or stamina to start runs, paid revives, paid reshuffles, real-time timers. These are the top complaints across all three Pocket Mines and Diggy's Adventure.
- Random card hands before a level.
- Collectibles whose duplicates do nothing.
- Auto-scroll and cluttered HUDs.
- Monsters that turn mining into whack-a-mole. Keep them few, with a telegraphed sight range, and let explosions solve them.
- Exponential upgrade prices that force grinding (Pocket Mine 2, Wall World).
- Heavy death penalties. Halving your cash (SteamWorld Dig) feels bad; losing the carried cargo is enough.
- Full material simulation: unreadable, and costly in Phaser.

**Per-planet mechanic ideas** that the research turned up and InkMine didn't use (yet):

| Idea | Source | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wind gusts push loose ore and crates sideways | Weather | InkCrab already has wind (`logic/wind.ts`) |
| Magnetism: a magnet pulls metal ore; rigged ore veins explode | Pocket Mine 2 Rigging Blasts | |
| Crates that drift up in low gravity, so you plan from below | DRG low gravity | Could join Pumice |
| Timed quakes with a visible warning | Motherload | Cardia's heartbeat uses this |

**Contracts and economy without grind**

- A primary objective plus optional secondaries (Deep Rock Galactic). Each contract should change one thing about how you play, not repeat the same fetch job.
- A clear pass/fail quota (Gold Miner). Spare money carries over to upgrades.
- Flat-ish upgrade prices, with new tiers arriving with new planets.
- Pay for skill, not repetition: streak and chain bonuses, first-clear contract pay. Replays are optional and pay less.
- Sale prices rise with depth and planet, so progress shows as income.

## Materials and depths: a realism pass (Oct 17, 2026)

A check of what each repair and gear piece is made of, and how deep each ore sits on Graphite, against real geology and engineering. **Applied Oct 17, 2026:** the four proposed recipes are in the game (`logic/repairs.ts`, `logic/blueprints.ts`), and the lore is in the spec's Graphite section. The rule: **keep the real order of things, compress the distances.** Real ore zones span hundreds of metres to kilometres; Graphite's whole world is 160 m deep, so the game squeezes depth by 10 to 50 times but keeps what lies above what. Facts marked *(unverified)* are from memory, not a source read for this pass.

### Depths

| Ore | Where now | Real world | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Graphite** | 4–60 m, rich seams near the surface | Mercury's dark surface is carbon: graphite was the only mineral light enough to float on its early magma ocean, so it formed a crust at the top (Peplowski et al., 2016) | ✅ Realistic, and it gives Graphite a real model: a small Mercury |
| **Water ice** | 1–18 m, thickest in the crater's shadow; 25–95 m deeper | Airless worlds keep ice in craters the sun never reaches, as on the Moon's and Mercury's poles *(unverified)*; ground ice can run deep, like Earth's permafrost *(unverified)* | ✅ Realistic |
| **Native copper** | 1–20 m | Native copper forms near the old water table, in the zone where groundwater enriched the ore (supergene enrichment) | ✅ Realistic, if Graphite once had groundwater. One line of lore covers it, and it fits the ice and the warm deep |
| **Galena** | 6–40 m | A primary sulfide, mostly below the old water table | ✅ Realistic |
| **Quartz** | 20–80 m, small crystals | The commonest mineral on Earth's continents, but dark, low-silica crusts like Mercury's or the Moon's have little free quartz *(unverified)*: clear crystals grow in veins | ✅ Realistic, with the lore that Graphite's crust is dark and low in silica |
| **Native silver** | 38–88 m | Native silver is most common near the surface, in the oxidised zone. It also forms in deep primary veins *(unverified: Kongsberg, Norway, mined to over 1 km)* | ⚠️ A gameplay choice. Keep it deep: it's the ore that pulls you down, and deep silver veins do exist |

### What the parts are made of

| Part | Now | Real world | Verdict | Proposed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Printer** | copper 6 | Printer hot-ends and wiring are copper or brass *(unverified)* | ✅ | Keep |
| **Radio** | copper 4, quartz 2 | Quartz crystals set radio frequencies; copper wires the set and the dish | ✅ | Keep |
| **Hull plating** | galena 10, graphite 6, "lead shields against radiation" | Lead is heavy, and when cosmic rays hit it, it sprays secondary radiation. Light, hydrogen- and carbon-rich materials shield better (NASA uses polyethylene as its reference). Carbon-fibre composites are real hull and structure material *(unverified)* | ❌ Lead hull is wrong | **graphite 10, galena 3**: carbon-composite panels, lead only to seal the seams. New fact: "Lead makes a poor hull: heavy, and hit by cosmic rays it sprays more radiation. Light carbon does better." |
| **Thrusters** | silver 6, quartz 4 | Engine chambers are lined with copper alloyed with a little silver (NARloy-Z, used in the RS-25 and RS-68), which carries heat away fast enough not to melt. Nozzle throats are often graphite *(unverified)*. Quartz has no part in a thruster | ⚠️ Silver right, quartz wrong | **copper 6, silver 6, graphite 2**. New fact: "Real rocket chambers are lined with copper and a little silver: it pulls the heat away before the wall can melt." |
| **Drive core** | 10 silver, from the Lamplighters | Invented technology | ✅ Fiction is fine here | Keep |
| **Cell** | graphite 4, copper 6 | Lithium-ion batteries: graphite anode coated on copper foil *(unverified)* | ✅ Spot on | Keep |
| **Pack** | graphite 3, copper 8 | A frame and fittings: mostly structure | ⚠️ Too much copper | **graphite 6, copper 2**: a carbon-fibre frame, copper fittings |
| **Drill** | graphite 6, galena 4 | Lead is far too soft for a drill. Electric motors use copper windings and graphite brushes; industrial diamond is made by pressing graphite *(unverified)* | ❌ Lead in a drill | **graphite 6, copper 4**: copper windings, graphite brushes and diamond grit for the bit |
| **Helmet** | graphite 4, quartz 2 | Spacecraft windows are fused silica, which is melted quartz *(unverified)* | ✅ | Keep |
| **Jetpack** | graphite 5, silver 3 | Graphite nozzle throat, silver in brazed joints *(unverified)* | ✅ Plausible | Keep |
| **Suit** | graphite 8, quartz 3 | Apollo suits had an outer layer of glass-fibre cloth ("Beta cloth") *(unverified)* | ✅ | Keep |

### What the changes do to play

- **The chapter still flows.** The Drill moves from galena to copper; both lie in the soil, so it can still be printed as soon as its blueprint is found. Thrusters still need silver from deep down, so the deep trip stays.
- **Graphite gets a tension.** It's the drill's fuel, and now the hull's main material too: burn it or build with it. That suits the planet named after it.
- **Galena and quartz are needed less.** Galena keeps the hull seals and the modules (winch, cargo hold, hull refit); quartz keeps the radio, helmet and suit. Both are plentiful, so nothing runs short, but a few veins of each could move to graphite.
- **Modules, briefly:** the winch's lead counterweight and copper motor are plausible. The cargo hold and the hull refit lean on lead; carbon panels would fit better there too.
